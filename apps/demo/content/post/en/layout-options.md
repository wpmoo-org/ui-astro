This Post inherits the common demo Sidebar and selects a wider content rail
through its own Layout options. The announcement separately disables its
Sidebar without changing this entry or the Archive.

## Start with the reading experience

A useful article layout gives the reader a clear place to begin. The title
introduces the subject, the description explains what to expect, and the
publication date stays secondary. Once the article starts, the text should
feel quieter than the page header without becoming difficult to read.

Longer paragraphs make the available space easier to judge. A short fixture
can look comfortable at almost any width, but several sentences quickly
reveal whether a line asks the reader to travel too far. The same content
should remain comfortable when the navigation opens, when the window narrows,
and when the page is read on a phone.

## Keep layout choices independent

The application Sidebar and an article's own table of contents have different
jobs. The Sidebar helps readers move around the site. A table of contents
helps them move within a longer article. An article does not need either one
to remain readable.

- Keep the title and introductory description together.
- Give the article body a predictable reading width.
- Preserve the order of the content on small screens.
- Show optional information only when it has been authored.

> Good defaults should make an ordinary article comfortable to read before
> a site adds its own branding or special sections.

### Let the content set the pace

Headings divide an article into ideas rather than decorating every paragraph.
Lists make a sequence easier to scan, while a short quotation can pause the
flow without becoming a second page header. These elements should still feel
like parts of one continuous article.

Links also belong to that reading flow. You can compare this article with
[the announcement without a Sidebar](/posts/announcement), or return to
[the Post archive](/posts) to see how the title and description work in a list.

## Check the narrow layout first

On a small screen, the reader should not have to choose between a useful
image and readable text. An archive can stack its image above the summary
until the available content rail is wide enough for both. The decision should
follow the usable space, not assume that a tablet always has a desktop layout.

1. Read the title without horizontal scrolling.
2. Follow a paragraph from beginning to end.
3. Open the navigation and confirm that the article remains accessible.
4. Switch between light and dark themes without losing the text hierarchy.

The goal is not to make every page identical. A contact page, an archive and
a long article serve different purposes. Shared defaults should provide a
coherent foundation while leaving those intentional differences intact.
